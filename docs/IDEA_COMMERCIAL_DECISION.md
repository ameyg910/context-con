# Commercial decision: vendor claims, LinkedIn evidence and alternatives

Reviewed 2026-11-22. This is a proposal and source review, not customer validation or a decision to implement a pivot. Two prior probes established retrieval possibilities and failure cases; they did not establish willingness to pay. No paid data searches or messages were made for this comparison. Free account metadata was checked.

## What Idea C actually replaces

A bounded public-evidence investigation could replace a defined analyst research assignment: answer specified vendor questions, cite exact evidence, identify unresolved questions and design the next verification step. It does not replace complete technical diligence, security approval, implementation testing or a purchase decision.

[ZeroFold's own service description](https://zerofold.co/solutions/technical-scientific-due-diligence) includes architecture, code, scientific/technical evidence, execution, specialist assessment and data-room/management access. This demonstrates an existing service category and the larger scope of that work; it does not validate demand for our smaller automated report.

The commercial gap is specific: no prospective buyer has supplied a recent paid research brief, accepted our report in place of that work, or committed to paying. Good citations alone do not resolve the gap. A specific research deliverable can nevertheless be a complete paid job; it need not always end in vendor approval. The event explicitly has a research track. The request and pass mark must be defined honestly.

## LinkedIn: a useful additional evidence route

Crustdata documents searches over current/past employer, title, dates and employment descriptions, plus profile enrichment. Its social search supports post text/date and author identity/company context. These can locate people who describe relevant projects and potentially identify actual customer users. [People reference](https://docs.crustdata.com/person-docs/search/reference), [person enrichment](https://docs.crustdata.com/person-docs/enrichment/reference.md), [social reference](https://docs.crustdata.com/social-post-docs/indexed-search/reference)

Observed account access on 2026-11-22: indexed people search/enrichment and indexed social search enabled; live professional-network people/social retrieval, contact enrichment and batch person verification disabled. A documented endpoint is not necessarily available to this key. [Official access reference](https://docs.crustdata.com/general/permissions.md)

Proposed collection sequence:

1. Search a small number of relevant current and former employees by company ID and role/description. Select only needed public professional fields.
2. Search original posts from identified authors or use topic/company searches as discovery. Do not require the current-employer filter to recover posts about a previous employer.
3. Match the project/post date to employment history and company/product identity. Preserve ambiguous timelines.
4. Follow referenced engineering articles, release documentation, public code or customer-side material. Check what the artifact actually demonstrates.
5. Label the conclusion precisely: employee-reported implementation; public artifact supports a component; customer-reported use; independently observed execution; or cannot verify.
6. Deduplicate copied announcements and reposts. Multiple employees repeating one launch are not multiple independent measurements.

Hypothetical example: a person's first-person description of migrating an ETA service to Kubernetes is evidence of their reported participation in a historical implementation. It does not establish today's full stack, model ownership, customer savings or permission to access private systems. An employee claiming a 40% improvement does not resolve the denominator or measurement methodology by itself.

Profile descriptions and posts can be valuable, specific and self-reported at the same time. A stronger search should be evaluated on incremental supported claims and useful expert leads, not on how many profiles it collects. Private/confidential work and contact details are not implied by public professional visibility. Employment alone is not authority to speak for the company.

The indexed social dataset can lag source publication and be rebuilt periodically. Check record dates and do not sell this account as guaranteed real-time LinkedIn monitoring. No-result queries cannot establish that a team, skill or implementation is absent. [Freshness](https://docs.crustdata.com/general/data-updates.md), [social dataset behavior](https://docs.crustdata.com/social-post-docs/indexed-search/reference#dataset-freshness)

## Three possible service businesses

### 1. 3PL sales qualification desk — provisional first choice for client/data fit

**Buyer:** the logistics client's head of sales/business development. **Existing service analogue:** outsourced prospect research, lead qualification and appointment setting. Callbox describes logistics clients dividing early-stage prospecting work between an outside agency and an internal sales team. These are provider-published accounts, not independently audited results or proof of Indian demand. [Callbox logistics case](https://www.callboxinc.com/case-studies/logistics-brand-seals-3-high-value-deals/)

**Proposed brief:** find companies with a recent, relevant logistics requirement that this 3PL can actually serve, identify the appropriate professional and establish what remains to qualify before handing over the opportunity.

**Inputs:** the client's supported lanes/sites, modes, capacity, certifications, exclusions, minimum commercial criteria and existing accounts. These are supplied operational facts, not Crustdata fields. An inbound lead/contact list or an authorized contact channel avoids depending on the disabled contact add-on.

**Data:** company search/identity; jobs and public announcements for candidate events; social posts for explicit requests or project context; people search for relevant roles; public primary sources for the underlying event. For physical site/capacity details, retrieve actual facility evidence instead of using company headquarters as a substitute. [Company search](https://docs.crustdata.com/company-docs/search/reference.md), [jobs](https://docs.crustdata.com/job-docs/search/reference.md), [people](https://docs.crustdata.com/person-docs/search/reference), [social](https://docs.crustdata.com/social-post-docs/indexed-search/reference)

**Differentiation to test:** reject unserviceable opportunities; distinguish planned expansion, completed expansion and an explicit open procurement need; explain fit against actual client rules; reconcile duplicates; qualify missing facts through an authorized conversation; deliver an accepted handoff/meeting rather than a list.

An expansion announcement is a research trigger, not confirmed intent. A completed warehouse may already have a supplier, and a logistics job may indicate internal operations. Lane, volume, incumbent, outsourcing need, budget and timing remain unknown until sourced or confirmed. Do not fabricate the commercial opportunity.

**Finished result:** for a discovery contract, a clearly bounded research shortlist with evidence and unknowns. For a qualification/appointment contract, captured qualifying answers and a recorded handoff or booking accepted by the relevant people. These are different service scopes. A drafted email cannot be reported as a booked meeting.

**Competition:** Clay already markets logistics lead generation and Apollo markets signals/intent workflows. A list plus a generated message has weak differentiation. Our hypothesis depends on logistics-specific qualification and completion. [Clay logistics workflow](https://www.clay.com/tools/generate-leads-in-logistics), [Apollo buying-intent overview](https://knowledge.apollo.io/hc/en-us/articles/8047704465933-Buying-Intent-Overview)

**Risks:** stale/sparse Indian coverage, speculative need, no contact enrichment, reply latency, crowded tools and missing operational client data. Outbound reply/booking cannot be guaranteed in a short run. An inbound qualification brief would be easier to bound if that is the released client request, although company data may be a smaller part of its value.

### 2. Logistics expert sourcing and screening — strongest distinctive use of project experience

**Buyer:** a 3PL operations/project leader seeking someone with relevant hands-on experience. **Service analogue:** expert-network sourcing, vetting and scheduling. AlphaSights describes sourcing experts to project scope and managing expert interactions; its public expert page distinguishes participation/availability from simply having relevant professional experience. [Client service](https://www.alphasights.com/product/client-service/), [expert participation](https://www.alphasights.com/experts/)

**Hypothetical brief:** find an operator who has opened a temperature-controlled distribution facility in India, screen for relevant responsibilities and current availability, and arrange an introductory consultation.

**Data:** employer and project descriptions, professional dates, certifications when permitted, original project posts and public artifacts. Search claimed project experience rather than just titles. Use the same-role matching semantics correctly; two requirements in different career roles may not establish experience on one project. [People reference](https://docs.crustdata.com/person-docs/search/reference), [enrichment](https://docs.crustdata.com/person-docs/enrichment/reference.md)

**Finished result:** screened, interested and available experts with agreed consultation scope, fee, relevant conflicts and a confirmed first call. A profile shortlist is only the sourcing stage of that larger job. Preserve self-reported versus corroborated experience. Do not claim availability, willingness, language proficiency, fee or permission to discuss employer information from a profile.

**Why consider it:** directly turns public achievement claims into specific sourcing leads, with an identifiable human-service analogue. It preserves some Idea C work by locating relevant operators/customer references instead of stretching public evidence into a technical verdict.

**Risks:** this fictional client has not stated such a need; uncertain frequency/budget; contacts and willing respondents; conflict/confidentiality checks; project claims still require screening. Access to people data does not replace the trust and consent parts of an expert network.

### 3. Vendor evidence research — keep only with a specific buyer question

Reuse the two completed probes if the released request asks for a sourced comparison or precise answer about suppliers. Narrow the scope to a decision such as documented integration compatibility and unresolved evidence. Require the buyer to identify the decision and acceptance criteria. Do not make full technical verification, vendor clearance or fraud detection the default promise.

Its advantages are existing progress and a traceable partial-result path. Its disadvantages are unclear standalone purchasing demand, a limited Crustdata-specific advantage and dependence on public documentation coverage. Additional LinkedIn searches might improve evidence but will not validate demand.

## Event constraints and a practical decision gate

The supplied [problem statement](C:/Users/goyal/Downloads/ContextCon05_BITS_Problem_Statement.pdf), pages 1–4, specifies a fixed fictional Indian 3PL, allows other jobs within tracks, and includes both revenue and research work. It requires sourced recent records/events, limits, approvals and restart behavior. Crustdata is optional. Financial feasibility is 25 points. Exact released client request/input-output formats still determine compatibility; no idea here is guaranteed eligible for an unseen request.

Do not choose a service merely to maximize Crustdata calls. Its people/company/jobs/social data naturally fit discovery and professional sourcing; they do not provide shipment rates, fleet capacity, insurance validity, live location, bank records or proof of actual buying intent. Specialized operational/official/client sources are needed for those decisions. This is a fit assessment, not a claim that Crustdata has exclusive access to professional data.

**Provisional recommendation:** prioritize the 3PL sales qualification desk if the client brief supports revenue work and supplies service constraints. Choose expert sourcing if a concrete operational expertise/hiring request is available and differentiation is the priority. Retain Idea C only for an actual bounded research request, rather than expanding its evidence collection to justify a generic product.

Before implementation, obtain a recent comparable job from the relevant buyer: the original brief, current process, accepted deliverable and what makes it payable. Test the same brief with our workflow. For sales, measure accepted qualified handoffs versus an ordinary company/contact search, and record disqualified cases. For expert sourcing, measure whether the candidates match the project and whether one agrees to a screened call. For C, ask what decision the report changed and what research the buyer could stop repeating. These are proposed validation steps; no buyer acceptance or prices were established today.
